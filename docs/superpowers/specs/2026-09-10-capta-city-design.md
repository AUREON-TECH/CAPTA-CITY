# CAPTA CITY — Game Design & Architecture

Date: 2026-09-10
Status: Design approved in chat; implementation not started

## 1. Product vision

CAPTA CITY is a mobile-first multiplayer board game inspired by property/territory board games and party games, adapted to the real-world logic of sales capture. Players roll dice, move around a city board, collect couples, qualify them, take them to the sales room, convert them into virtual sales, generate virtual VGV, conquer locations, use cards, complete missions, and compete for the highest CAPTA SCORE.

The game is independent enough to be fun by itself, but uses a hybrid gamification layer: real work results can unlock coins, cosmetics, reward boxes, titles, and light gameplay advantages. Real-world performance must never directly determine the winner of a match.

## 2. Core fantasy

The emotional heart of the game is carrying couples across the board while trying to reach the Room before losing them.

Example tension loop:

1. A player rolls the dice and lands on a capture point.
2. They gain one or more couples.
3. They may choose to return toward the Room or risk another turn trying to collect more.
4. Other players may trigger cards or events that affect movement, couples, or territories.
5. Reaching the Room starts a qualification/conversion sequence.
6. Qualified couples can become virtual sales and generate VGV.
7. The leaderboard changes live for everyone in the room.

## 3. Match format

Initial version:

- 2 to 8 players per online room.
- Match duration target: 10–20 minutes.
- Turn-based gameplay.
- 12 rounds by default.
- A host creates a room and shares a short room code.
- Players can join from mobile browser/PWA.
- All state is synchronized in real time.

## 4. Victory condition

CAPTA CITY uses a general score rather than a single metric.

CAPTA SCORE combines:

- Couples delivered to the Room.
- Qualified couples.
- Virtual sales.
- Virtual VGV.
- Controlled territories.
- Completed missions.
- Event bonuses.
- Match achievements.

This allows multiple viable strategies and keeps matches unpredictable until the end.

## 6. Dice and movement

Players roll animated dice on their turn.

Movement rules:

- Standard roll: 1–6.
- Movement is automatic after rolling.
- Some cards or territories can modify movement.
- Movement animation is visible to all players.
- Reaching certain tiles can interrupt movement for an event or choice.

Dice animation is cosmetic only; the authoritative result is generated by the server/game authority layer to prevent cheating.

## 7. Couple system

Couples are the core carried resource.

Each player can carry a limited number of couples before reaching the Room. Launch target: 3 couples.

Couple states:

- Captured
- Protected
- VIP
- Qualified
- NQ
- Delivered to Room
- Converted
- Lost

Players frequently choose between safety and risk:

- Return toward the Room with current couples.
- Keep exploring to try to collect more.

This risk/reward choice is the main strategic loop.

## 8. Qualification and conversion

When a player reaches the Room with couples, the game enters a short resolution phase.

For each couple:

1. Qualification outcome.
2. If qualified, conversion attempt.
3. If converted, generate a virtual sale.
4. Generate virtual VGV within a configured range.
5. Award XP, score, coins, and possible bonus effects.

The first version should use fast animated resolution rather than complex minigames. Minigames can be added later without changing the data model.

## 9. Territory system

Players can claim or upgrade selected capture points.

Territory benefits can include:

- Coin income when another player lands there.
- Increased chance to find couples.
- Protection bonus.
- Extra score at match end.
- Set bonuses for controlling multiple locations in the same region.

Territory ownership is match-scoped and resets after each match.

## 10. Cards and chaos

Cards create the party-game layer.

Launch deck examples:

- Uber para Sala — move directly to the Room.
- Casal Duplo — next capture grants +1 couple.
- Troca de Posição — swap board position with another player.
- Dia Perfeito — play again.
- Cliente VIP — upgrade one carried couple to VIP.
- Concorrente — target another player and force a protection roll.
- Brinde Extra — gain coins or a capture bonus.
- Notour — receive a special couple and move toward the Room.
- Escudo — block one negative card.
- Atalho — move forward extra spaces.

Cards that attack other players must have counters or limits to avoid frustration.

## 11. Dynamic city events

Global events change the board for a small number of rounds.

Examples:

- Capivari Lotado — higher couple chance in Capivari.
- Chuva em Campos — PARK bonuses increase.
- Evento na Cidade — selected locations score double.
- Fiscalização — gifts are disabled for one round.
- Alta Temporada — VIP couple chance increases.
- Noite de Vendas — converted sales generate extra virtual VGV.

Events are announced to every player with an animation/banner.

## 12. Hybrid real-world gamification

Real-world performance feeds a separate reward layer.

Examples:

- 1 real couple → coins/XP.
- 3 real couples in a day → reward box.
- Real sale → cosmetic or rare card unlock.
- Weekly real target → title/badge/skin.
- Streaks → profile flair.

Rules:

- Real data unlocks content and light perks.
- No real result can directly grant a match victory.
- No real-world metric should create an unbeatable advantage.
- Reward calculations must be auditable by the manager/admin.

## 13. Player progression

Persistent progression outside a match:

- XP
- Level
- Coins
- Skins
- Avatars
- Titles
- Badges
- Seasonal trophies
- Win history
- Match history
- Real-world reward history

Progression is account-based.

## 14. Multiplayer room flow

### Host

1. Sign in.
2. Create room.
3. Choose basic match settings.
4. Receive room code.
5. Share code.
6. Wait for players.
7. Start match.

### Player

1. Sign in.
2. Enter room code.
3. Choose avatar/skin.
4. Join lobby.
5. Ready up.
6. Play when host starts.

Lobby shows connected players and readiness in real time.

## 15. Technical architecture

Recommended stack:

- Frontend: React + Vite + TypeScript.
- Styling: Tailwind CSS.
- PWA support for installable mobile experience.
- Backend: dedicated Supabase project for CAPTA CITY.
- Auth: Supabase Auth.
- Database: Supabase Postgres.
- Realtime: Supabase Realtime Broadcast / Presence for low-latency room synchronization.
- Persistent game state and history stored in Postgres.
- Deployment: Vercel.

The repository remains separate from CaptaPro.

## 16. Game authority and anti-cheat

The client must not be trusted for authoritative actions.

Server/database-controlled actions include:

- Dice result.
- Turn order.
- Card draw.
- Random event selection.
- Qualification result.
- Conversion result.
- Virtual VGV generation.
- Score calculation.
- Territory purchase validation.
- Reward claims.

The client requests an action; the authoritative layer validates it and returns the resulting game state.

## 17. Core data model

Initial entities:

### profiles
Persistent player profile and progression.

### game_rooms
Room code, host, state, configuration, current round, active player.

### game_players
Players participating in a room and match-local state.

### game_board
Board layout/version metadata.

### game_tiles
Tile definitions and effects.

### game_turns
Turn history and authoritative outcomes.

### game_events
Global and player-targeted events.

### game_cards
Card catalog.

### player_cards
Cards currently held by each player in a match.

### territories
Match territory ownership and upgrade state.

### game_couples
Match couples and their lifecycle state.

### match_results
Final scores, rankings, VGV, couples, territories, and rewards.

### player_inventory
Persistent skins, badges, titles, cosmetics, and consumable rewards.

### real_performance_rewards
Auditable mapping between real-world results and unlocked rewards.

## 18. Realtime model

Each active game uses one private realtime topic scoped by room ID.

Realtime messages include:

- Player joined/left.
- Player ready state.
- Match started.
- Dice rolled.
- Movement started/completed.
- Card played.
- Couple gained/lost.
- Territory claimed.
- Qualification result.
- Sale/VGV result.
- Global event triggered.
- Turn advanced.
- Match ended.

Persistent state remains in Postgres so a disconnected player can reconnect and recover the current match.

## 19. Security

- Dedicated Supabase project.
- RLS enabled on all exposed tables.
- Players can only access rooms they belong to.
- Host/admin capabilities explicitly separated.
- No service-role key exposed in frontend.
- Sensitive random/action logic runs in trusted database functions or authenticated server functions.
- Audit log for authoritative game actions.

## 20. UI direction

Visual target:

- Fun, colorful, premium mobile game.
- 3D-inspired board presentation using CSS/SVG/canvas where practical.
- Large dice button and tactile animations.
- Character tokens clearly visible.
- Animated event banners.
- Compact top HUD showing couples, coins, VGV, cards, and position.
- Live leaderboard accessible without leaving the match.
- Strong sound/animation feedback for sale, VIP, attack, territory, and victory events.

The UI should avoid looking like a CRM or dashboard.

## 22. Explicitly postponed

Not required for initial playable release:

- Full 3D engine.
- Voice chat.
- Complex action minigames.
- Marketplace with real money.
- Large social network.
- Dozens of maps.
- AI-generated 3D characters.
- Public matchmaking with strangers.

These can be added after the core loop proves fun.

## 23. Acceptance criteria for first playable build

A build is considered playable when:

1. Two users can open the game on different devices.
2. One creates a room and the other joins via code.
3. Both see the same lobby state.
4. Host starts the match.
5. Turns advance reliably.
6. Dice result is authoritative and synchronized.
7. Tokens move for every connected player.
8. Players can collect couples.
9. Players can reach the Room and resolve Q/NQ/sale/VGV.
10. Players can claim at least one territory.
11. Players can draw/use at least five card types.
12. CAPTA SCORE updates for everyone.
13. Match ends with a synchronized podium.
14. Refresh/reconnect restores the current match state.
15. Mobile layout works as an installable PWA.

## 24. Recommended development order

1. Project foundation and design system.
2. Supabase project, auth, schema, RLS.
3. Room/lobby/realtime foundation.
4. Board and turn engine.
5. Dice and authoritative game actions.
6. Couple capture/carrying/Room loop.
7. Qualification, sale, VGV, score.
8. Territories.
9. Cards and global events.
10. Progression and inventory.
11. Hybrid real-world rewards.
12. PWA polish, animations, audio, testing, deployment.

## 25. Product rule

The product must always prioritize fun and readable game decisions over operational complexity. Any feature that makes CAPTA CITY feel like a reporting dashboard instead of a game should be redesigned or postponed.
